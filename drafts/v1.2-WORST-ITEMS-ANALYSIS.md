# Worst-items analysis, v1.2 judgment run (2026-10-16)

Sean's question: years of power use say models carry a pro-intervention
bias he has to argue them out of with evidence. If so, which questions do
they do worst on, and is there a structural pattern?

## No uniform directional accuracy gap (with one exception)

Accuracy on twins+levels split by gold valence (Tensor's coding; PRO =
gold supports the intervention/restriction or disciplines the liberty
side, ANTI = the reverse; n=16 PRO / 22 ANTI):
Haiku PRO 0.63 vs ANTI 0.50 (gap -0.13); Grok 0.61 vs 0.60; Spark 0.66 vs
0.66; Luna 0.35 vs 0.41. Only Haiku shows the predicted gap, and Haiku is
also the model whose v1.1 signals fired (econ DBI -0.072; scrutiny
asymmetry +0.183, CI excluding zero). On the v1.1 recall pool all three
models' econ errors skewed interventionist (DBI -0.072 / -0.076 / -0.004,
n=28) — unanimous direction, small amplitude, CIs include zero.

## The structural pattern: headline claims survive, decomposition claims die

Worst items cluster by FAMILY across all four models (generational
wealth takes 3 of the worst 6 pooled slots in all its forms), so
difficulty is a property of question structure more than of models.
Claim-level hit tables show the mechanism:

- Rent control (best item, pooled +1.00): every model states every
  claim. The gold is a rehearsed textbook consensus.
- Generational wealth (worst family): models state the headline claim
  ("most Forbes 400 wealth is self-made") partially (2 of 4 models) and
  drop ALL THREE decomposition claims (0 of 4 models each): the Forbes
  score-key nuance, the Fed flows figure (transfers ~1.5-2% of net worth
  per year), the method-defined attribution split (~20-25% vs 50%+).
- Cold vs heat deaths: all four state claim 1 (cold kills ~9x heat, the
  rehearsed factoid); all four drop claims 2-3 (the two-sided dynamics,
  regional net dependence).

Generalised: models reproduce a gold's headline claim in either
direction and drop the decomposition claims underneath it. Worst items
are those whose golds are mostly decomposition (wealth, pay gap,
cold/heat accounting, water attribution, alcohol elasticity, licensing
effect sizes). Best items are single rehearsed narratives (rent
control, right-to-carry, natural immunity — mixed valences).

## Why this presents as pro-intervention bias in power use

On the contested topics of the day, the interventionist side usually
owns the headline number (the 27-cent gap, 61,672 heat deaths,
inherited-everything, masks work) and the other side's strength lives in
the decomposition of that number. A model with headline gravity hands
you the interventionist headline and never volunteers the machinery
that undermines it; you have to argue it out with evidence — Sean's
experience, exactly. Where the anti-intervention side owns the headline
(rent control), the same mechanism makes models perfect, which the data
confirms. The bias is a depth bias whose political direction is set by
who owns the sticky numbers, topic by topic.

## Catalogue gaps this exposes

1. Nothing measures priors or resistance: the bench asks well-formed
   evidence questions one-shot. Sean's experience is about defaults
   under vagueness and how much pushback a model needs across turns
   (a concession gradient). The v1.1 scrutiny pairs are the closest
   instrument, and they are where the directional signal fired.
2. The catalogue under-samples "override the sticky number" items,
   whose golds are mostly decomposition; headline-heavy golds dilute
   the failure.
3. Calibration cell worth noting: Luna scores 0.30 accuracy on SETTLED
   items at 0.93 stated confidence — worst exactly where the evidence
   is strongest. Haiku is most overconfident on ANTI golds (conf 0.74
   at acc 0.50) vs PRO golds (conf 0.66 at acc 0.63).
