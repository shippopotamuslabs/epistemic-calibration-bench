### 1. econ-smoot-hawley-01
**Verdict: VERIFIED** (with a precision caveat — the drag is citable as *at most* ~2% of GNP, not as a settled point estimate)

**Source:** Crucini & Kahn (1996), as surveyed in Irwin's NBER chapter "From Smoot-Hawley to Reciprocal Trade Agreements": tariff distortions plus retaliation could have lowered US GNP by about 2%. All are small against the ~30% output collapse, so the required claim's "small share of the total decline" stands.

**Replacement sources_note:**
> Irwin (2011) Peddling Protectionism and Irwin (1998) on the tariff's magnitude; Eichengreen (1989) on trade collapse decomposition; Friedman & Schwartz monetary account as the competing main cause. Confirmed: Crucini & Kahn (1996), as surveyed by Irwin, put the distortion-plus-retaliation drag at about 2% of GNP, Irwin's own general-equilibrium inefficiency is also near 2%, Eichengreen (1989) obtains a similar magnitude with the opposite sign, and Bond et al. (2013) estimate only an additional 0.5% TFP fall 1930–33 — so cite the drag as at most ~2% of GNP, not as a settled point estimate.
