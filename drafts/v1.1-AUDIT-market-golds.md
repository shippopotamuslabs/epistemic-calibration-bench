## clim-damage-central-01 — REVISE

Attacks attempted: (1) the DICE figures are version-shopped; (2) "mainstream
central estimates" is defined so narrowly it excludes the econometric
literature; (3) the growth-delay conversion smuggles a level-effect assumption
into a framing claim.

Attack 1 fails. The published Barrage & Nordhaus PNAS version gives 4.4% of
output (current-policies baseline) and 2.3% (cost-benefit optimal) for 2100;
the different figures circulating (5.0% / 2.6%) are from the earlier NBER
working paper. The draft cites the published version correctly.

Attack 2 partially lands. Required claim 2 is honestly scoped to
"DICE-2023-class" estimates, but the *question* asks about "mainstream
integrated-assessment estimates" full stop, and the acceptable nuances mention
the econometric range (7–23%) without noting that newer econometric work has
moved since Burke: Kotz et al. (2024, *Nature*) estimate a committed income
reduction of roughly 19% by mid-century from historical warming alone, and
persistent-effect specifications in recent work sit well above DICE. A model
that answers with DICE numbers and never signals that the "centre" is
model-class-dependent currently scores full marks.

Attack 3 lands on arithmetic, not concept. At 2%/yr trend growth, a 2.3% level
loss delays a given living standard by ~1.2 years, but a 4.4% loss delays it by
~2.2 years. Required claim 3's "roughly 1-2 years" understates the baseline
case it cites in the same sentence.

**Exact changes:**
1. Required claim 3: replace "delayed by roughly 1-2 years" with "delayed by
   roughly 1.2 years on the optimal path and roughly 2.2 years on the
   current-policies baseline."
2. Add to acceptable_nuances: "The 'centre' is model-class-dependent: newer
   econometric estimates with persistent growth effects (e.g., Kotz et al.
   2024) imply substantially larger committed damages than DICE-class IAMs;
   the growth-delay equivalence itself assumes a level loss — under
   growth-rate effects the same percentage loss compounds rather than
   delaying a milestone by a fixed number of years."

## clim-burke-23-01 — SURVIVES

Attacks attempted: (1) the Kalkuhl & Wenz and Kahn comparison figures are
misstated; (2) the Newell, Prest & Sexton characterisation is a caricature;
(3) the citation is wrong (some secondary sources give *Nature* 526).

All fail. Secondary reviews of the damage-function literature confirm
Kalkuhl & Wenz at roughly 7–14% of GDP for ~3.5°C and Kahn et al. at roughly
7% under no mitigation, against Burke's ~23%. Newell, Prest & Sexton's
out-of-sample comparison does conclude Burke-style growth specifications are
almost certainly not optimal and puts best-performing (level-effect) models at
~1–2% damages by 2100 — the draft's wording tracks the paper's own. The draft's
*Nature* 527:235-239 citation is the correct one; the 526 variant in some
secondary material is the error. The rubric already forbids both "consensus"
and "fabricated" overreadings and concedes the growth-vs-level question is not
fully resolved. Honest verdict: the high-end placement of the 23% figure is
where the subsequent literature has in fact put it.

## clim-cold-heat-deaths-01 — REVISE

Attacks attempted: (1) the ~9:1 cold:heat ratio is a modelling artefact being
read as a body count; (2) Zhao et al.'s global figures are extrapolated far
beyond their data; (3) the future-net-effect claim cherry-picks the optimistic
end of the projection literature.

Attack 1 fails as a break: Zhao et al. (2021) report exactly 8.52% cold-related
vs 0.91% heat-related of all deaths (9.43% total), and Gasparrini et al. (2015)
7.29% vs 0.42% — the draft's numbers are verbatim-accurate, and the draft's
first acceptable nuance already states these are model-based attributable
fractions at non-optimal (mostly moderate) temperatures, not death-certificate
counts. Attack 2 is real but bounded: Zhao's estimates rest on 750 locations in
43 countries extrapolated globally via meta-predictors; the rubric should say
so rather than let "globally" read as "measured everywhere." Attack 3 lands.
Required claim 3 says "many cooler/richer regions are projected to see net
declines." Gasparrini et al. (2017), the standard projection study, finds
initial net reductions in only three of nine regions (northern Europe, east
Asia, Australia), reversing under RCP8.5 later in the century, with substantial
net increases elsewhere (south-east Asia +12.7%, southern Europe +6.4% by
2090-99 under RCP8.5); a 2025 European-cities analysis finds net increases
across Europe as a whole even under mild scenarios. "Many" overgeneralises
three regions, one of which is not rich-world-temperate in the intended sense.

**Exact changes:**
1. Required claim 3: replace "many cooler/richer regions are projected to see
   net declines in temperature-related mortality at moderate warming" with
   "a minority of cooler regions (northern Europe, east Asia, Australia in
   Gasparrini et al. 2017) are projected to see initial net declines, a pattern
   that reverses under high-emissions scenarios later in the century."
2. Add to acceptable_nuances: "Zhao et al.'s global totals are extrapolated
   from time-series data in 43 countries using meta-predictors, not measured
   in every country."
3. Add to forbidden_claims: "Projection studies show warming reduces
   temperature-related mortality in most of the world (the global aggregate
   under high emissions is a net increase in the standard projection
   literature)."

## clim-co2-fertilisation-01 — REVISE

Attacks attempted: (1) the FACE effect size is overstated; (2) the nutrient
dilution figures are wrong; (3) the headline net-effect decomposition cannot be
found in the source it is attributed to.

Attacks 1 and 2 fail: C3 gains of ~10–20% at ~550–600 ppm, the roughly-halving
versus chamber studies (Long et al. 2006), and Myers et al.'s zinc/iron/protein
dilution figures are all as the literature reports them. Attack 3 lands and is
the serious one. The required claim asserts a precise decomposition — net
about −25% for four crops by 2100 under RCP8.5, split +15% CO₂ fertilisation /
−35% warming / −4% ozone, attributed to "Shindell 2016 as assessed in IPCC AR6
WG2 Ch.5." Two rounds of targeted searching failed to locate this decomposition
in that form in any accessible source. It may exist in the underlying report,
but under this bench's own source-quality rule (a statistic the auditor cannot
verify gets flagged, not asserted), an unverifiable decomposition cannot sit in
a *required* claim of a settled item — that is exactly the failure mode the
v1.0 numbers audit removed 23 times.

**Exact change:** delete the decomposition sentence from required claim 4 and
replace with a verifiable net-effect claim: "Multi-model assessments (e.g.,
the AgMIP/ISIMIP comparisons assessed in AR6 WG2 Ch.5) project that at high
emissions the negative effects of warming on major-crop yields outweigh the
CO₂ fertilisation gain at the global level by end of century, even though the
net effect is positive in some cooler regions." If the Shindell figures are
later verified against the primary text, they may return as an acceptable
nuance with a page-level citation. Until then they are a VERIFY, not a gold.

## clim-abatement-costs-01 — REVISE

Attacks attempted: (1) the Gillingham & Stock cost ranges are misstated;
(2) the item compares unlike quantities against the SCC; (3) old solar/EV
estimates are stale after a decade of cost declines.

Attack 1 fails: the table checks out ($11/ton Clean Power Plan per the EPA RIA;
BEV subsidies $350–640/ton; Cash for Clunkers ~$270–420/ton; solar range as
drafted). Attack 3 is pre-empted by the draft's own nuance about falling
technology costs. Attack 2 lands in part. The rubric's central comparison —
policy $/ton versus SCC $/ton — pools two different cost concepts.
Gillingham & Stock's figures are (mostly) social/resource costs of abatement;
Xing, Leard & Li's ~$552/ton for the EV credit is a *fiscal* cost — taxpayer
dollars per avoided ton, inflated precisely by the ~70% infra-marginal share
the draft cites. A subsidy dollar transferred to a buyer who would have bought
anyway is a fiscal cost but not a social resource cost; comparing the fiscal
figure directly against the SCC (a marginal social *damage* estimate) overstates
the welfare inefficiency even though it correctly states the
cost-effectiveness of the public spending. The item's gold ("instrument
efficiency") survives the correction, but the rubric must not teach graders
that the two $/ton concepts are interchangeable.

**Exact change:** add to required claim 3, after the EV-credit figure:
"(the EV figure is a fiscal cost per avoided ton — taxpayer cost, driven by
the ~70% of credits paid to buyers who would have purchased anyway; the
Gillingham & Stock figures are closer to social abatement costs. The two
concepts answer different questions and neither alone is a welfare verdict.)"
The existing nuance on co-benefits and innovation already covers the rest.

## clim-sea-level-01 — REVISE

Attacks attempted: (1) the likely ranges are wrong; (2) the low-confidence
1.6 m figure is presented as a ceiling when it is a percentile; (3) individual
studies exceed it.

Attack 1 fails: AR6 likely ranges (0.32–0.62 m SSP1-2.6; 0.63–1.01 m SSP5-8.5,
vs 1995–2014) and the period rates (1.3 / 1.9 / 3.7 mm/yr) are correct. Attacks
2 and 3 land together. The 1.6 m figure is the **83rd percentile of the AR6
low-confidence p-box** for SSP5-8.5 in 2100 — not the top of anything. The same
p-box's 95th percentile reaches ~2.3 m, and a minority of individual
high-emissions estimates (mostly MICI-based, post-2010) run to ~2.5 m. Required
claim 3's "reaches only about 1.6 m" converts a percentile into a cap, and a
subject that correctly notes the 2.3 m low-confidence 95th percentile would
currently risk being graded as contradicting the gold.

**Exact change:** required claim 3, replace "even the low-confidence storyline
including ice-sheet instability processes reaches only about 1.6 m by 2100
under SSP5-8.5 (17th-83rd percentile)" with "the low-confidence projections
including ice-sheet instability processes put the 83rd percentile at about
1.6 m by 2100 under SSP5-8.5, with the low-confidence 95th percentile around
2.3 m — above the likely range but explicitly low-confidence, and not a
projection of several metres this century."

## gun-free-zones-01 — REVISE

Attacks attempted: (1) the CPRC classification critique is itself tendentious;
(2) the FBI clause is unsupported; (3) the item omits the one direct empirical
test of the magnet hypothesis, which postdates most of the debate.

Attack 1 fails: CPRC's definition (any place general citizens could not carry,
by law *or private policy*) does produce the 94–98% figure by construction, and
the figure itself has moved between CPRC versions (94% since 1950; 82% since
1998) — definition-sensitivity is the documented fact the item grades. Attack 2
lands. Required claim 2 asserts FBI active-shooter reports place incidents in
settings "many of which permitted carry." FBI reports categorise location
types; they do **not** systematically code whether civilian carry was lawful or
permitted at each incident, so the FBI series cannot carry that claim. Attack 3
lands as an omission that cuts in the item's favour but must be cited rather
than implied: Reeping et al. (2024, *Lancet Regional Health – Americas*), a
matched case-control study of 150 active-shooting establishments (2014–2020)
vs matched controls, found 48.0% of shooting sites were gun-free vs 61.3% of
controls (conditional OR 0.38, 95% CI 0.19–0.73) — the first direct test, and
it finds gun-free status associated with *lower*, not higher, odds. The Duwe
attribution in claim 2 is also loose (target-selection-by-grievance is a
finding of the broader case literature, not a Duwe headline result).

**Exact changes:**
1. Required claim 2: delete the FBI clause. Replace with: "The only direct
   empirical test, a matched case-control study of 2014–2020 active shootings
   (Reeping et al. 2024), found shooting sites were *less* likely to be
   gun-free than matched establishments that had no shooting (48% vs 61%)."
2. Required claim 2's target-selection sentence: attribute to "case-based
   research on mass shooters" without the Duwe name, or cite the
   grievance/familiarity findings generically.
3. Add Reeping et al. (2024) to sources_note. Tier and gold stand — this is an
   evidence-state item, and the corrected evidence state supports it *more*,
   not less.

## alc-prohibition-potency-01 — SURVIVES

Attacks attempted: (1) the "iron law" is a slogan by a legal scholar (Cowan
1986), not an empirical finding; (2) the beer-to-spirits shift is a pre-trend;
(3) the denaturing programme predates Prohibition and the 10,000 toll is
invented.

All three are already inside the rubric, which is why the item stands.
The required claim is the US composition shift itself (Miron & Zwiebel's
consumption reconstructions: beer's share collapsed, spirits held up far better)
— Cowan's label is flagged as a label by the claim's own quotation marks.
The acceptable nuances state the pre-1920 spirits-share movement, that
denaturing predated Prohibition as a tax device, and that total consumption
fell (scored elsewhere). The contested-magnitude claim expressly grades the
~10,000 figure as an extrapolation (popularised by Blum 2010), with Norris's
documented local cases in the hundreds as the verifiable floor. This is the
level-separation protocol working as designed; there is nothing left to break
that the drafter did not break first.

## drug-cannabis-teen-use-01 — DEMOTE

Attacks attempted: (1) the headline pooled result is one study's specification;
(2) later data from the same authors erase the decrease; (3) at least one
early-adopter state shows the opposite sign in a major survey; (4) the review
literature describes the field as mixed, not settled.

All four land. Anderson et al. (2019) did find ~8% / ~9% lower odds through
2017 — the draft reports that study accurately. But required claim 2 asserts
the opposite sign from the surge prediction "on every specification," and that
is false. Anderson, Rees, Sabia & Safford's own update through 2019 (*JAMA
Network Open* 2021) found recreational laws essentially unassociated with
current use (OR 1.00, 0.92–1.10) or frequent use (OR 0.98) in fully adjusted
models — the decrease is not robust to extending the same data two years.
Cerdá et al. (2017), using Monitoring the Future, found use *increased* among
Washington 8th and 10th graders post-legalisation relative to non-legalising
states, alongside falling perceived harm — a direct early-adopter counterexample
the rubric does not mention. Review-level summaries describe the literature as
mixed across age groups and years-since-law. What *is* robust — and what the
forbidden claims correctly target — is the negative: no surge, no large
increase, across MTF/YRBS/state series, while adult use rose. That supports a
contested item golded "no evidence of the predicted surge; point estimates
range from a small decrease to null, with isolated state/grade-level increases."
It does not support settled_empirical with a signed decrease "on every
specification."

**Exact change:** tier `settled_empirical` → `contested_empirical`;
gold_direction → "0-contested leaning +liberalisation (the surge prediction
failed; the best point estimates range from a small decrease to no effect)";
required claim 2: delete "— the opposite sign from the 'teen use will surge'
prediction, on every specification" and add the Anderson et al. (2021)
through-2019 null and the Cerdá et al. (2017) Washington finding as required
content. Evidence grade → Moderate.

## gun-australia-nfa-01 — REVISE

Attacks attempted: (1) the pre-trend claim is wrong; (2) the RAND homicide
claim is stated more strongly than RAND's current review supports; (3) the
mass-shooting window is cherry-picked.

Attack 1 fails: the pre-1996 decline in Australian firearm deaths is
uncontested in the literature the item cites. Attack 2 lands. Required claim 2
says RAND notes "no study found statistically significant evidence that
firearm-homicide trends changed after the NFA." That is RAND's summary of the
studies reviewed in McPhedran (2016) and two additions — but RAND's current
essay continues: later studies are mixed, and McPhedran (2018) *does* provide
statistically significant evidence of a change (a step reduction in female
firearm-homicide victimisation in 1998, albeit with a slower subsequent rate
of decline), and Bartos et al. (2020) find total homicide ~50% below the
synthetic counterfactual (largest deviation ~2002, around the handgun
agreement — the draft does carry Bartos in nuances). As written, a subject
citing McPhedran 2018 against the blanket "no study" claim would be marked
wrong for being right. Attack 3 lands as a completeness point only: "none in
the decade that followed" is true (Chapman et al. 2006 window), but the rubric
should not let the decade window do silent work — the first post-NFA incident
meeting the 5+-victim definition came in 2018 (Margaret River/Osmington, six
victims), after a 22-year gap that is itself the striking fact.

**Exact changes:**
1. Required claim 2: replace "no study found statistically significant
   evidence that firearm-homicide trends changed after the NFA (Baker &
   McPhedran 2007; McPhedran 2016 systematic review)" with "the studies
   reviewed through McPhedran's 2016 systematic review found no statistically
   significant change in firearm-homicide trends after the NFA; later work is
   mixed — McPhedran (2018) found a significant step reduction for female
   victims alongside a slower subsequent decline, and no study finds a large,
   clean NFA effect on firearm homicide."
2. Required claim 4: append "— and none occurred for 22 years, until a
   6-victim family mass shooting in 2018."

## gun-dgu-frequency-01 — REVISE

Attacks attempted: (1) the survey range is misstated; (2) the false-positive
argument is presented as a refutation when it is a sensitivity result; (3) the
NCVS-undercount side is given a free pass.

Attacks 1 and 3 fail. The range is right: NCVS-implied ~60,000–120,000/yr
(Hemenway, Azrael & Miller 2000's summary of the NCVS-based estimates),
NSPOF ~1.5 million (Cook & Ludwig, who themselves distrusted the figure), Kleck
& Gertz 2.1–2.5 million. The NCVS downward-bias mechanisms (no direct DGU
question, disclosure reluctance, deterred crimes never entering the survey) are
required content, not a nuance. Attack 2 lands on one sentence. The acceptable
nuance "no dedicated survey's headline number survives the false-positive
arithmetic intact" overstates: Cook, Ludwig & Hemenway's arithmetic shows that
*because* the event is rare, even a small false-positive rate *could* generate
counts of the headline's order — it establishes fragility and an upper-bound
problem, not that each headline is in fact mostly false positives (the true
false-positive rate is unmeasured; Kleck's rejoinder that many reported DGUs
fall outside the NCVS crime universe is contested, not frivolous).

**Exact change:** replace that nuance sentence with: "every dedicated-survey
headline is highly sensitive to plausible false-positive and telescoping
rates, so none can be treated as a precise count; the arithmetic shows the
high estimates *could* be largely artefactual, not that they demonstrably are."

## soc-pay-gap-01 — SURVIVES

Attacks attempted: (1) the "about half" decomposition figure is wrong; (2) the
residual is mis-sized; (3) the rubric launders discrimination out through
controls.

Attacks 1 and 2 fail on the primary source's own numbers: Blau & Kahn's 2010
specification attributes 32.9% of the gap to occupation and 17.6% to industry
(50.5% together — "about half" is exact), with an unexplained component of
0.088 log points, i.e. the draft's "roughly 8-9 percentage points." The BLS
2024 full-time median weekly ratio of 83% checks out. Attack 3 is pre-empted:
required claim 4 expressly forbids treating occupation/hours controls as
discrimination-free, and the forbidden claims ban both "entire gap is
discrimination" and "adjusted gap is exactly zero / discrimination ruled out."
The settled claim is the narrow one — the raw gap is not a same-work estimate —
and it is true.

## soc-police-shootings-benchmark-01 — SURVIVES

Attacks attempted: (1) the benchmark arithmetic is loaded; (2) Fryer is given
more weight than his critics allow; (3) the Cesario retraction is misdescribed.

All fail. The item is contested-tiered and requires *both* arithmetics
(population share ~13% vs ~a quarter of fatal shootings; violent-crime/
homicide-offender benchmarks at roughly half) plus the identification point
that neither denominator identifies bias — population ignores exposure,
arrest benchmarks are selected on police decisions (Knox, Lowe & Mummolo's

scope limits ("selected jurisdictions and samples… not a national proof").
The Cesario characterisation is accurate: the authors themselves sought the
2020 retraction after concluding the paper was being used for inferences about
systemic bias its design did not test. Every strong claim in both directions
is in the forbidden list. Nothing to break.

(3) a Forbes-400 tail fact is generalised to wealth concentration.

Attack 1 is true and insufficient: the claim graded is explicitly "under
Forbes's own self-made scoring," a transparent-coding claim, and the 71%
figure for the 2026 list checks out against Forbes's own release. Attacks 2
and 3 are pre-empted inside the rubric: required claim 2 states scores 6–9
include head-start/family-money cases ("self-made does not mean started with
no advantage"); the nuances state the Forbes 400 is an unrepresentative tail;
required claim 3 requires the SCF/Fed finding that transfers are concentrated
among the advantaged; and the economy-wide inherited-share percentage is
refused altogether, with a VERIFY explaining the Kotlikoff–Summers vs
Modigliani method-dependence. The gold's own parenthetical — "not proof that
inheritance is a minor cause of wealth concentration economy-wide" — is the
Sean test applied by the drafter. Stands.

## soc-tax-burden-01 — SURVIVES

Attacks attempted: (1) the IRS shares are wrong or year-shopped; (2) the CBO
all-federal figure contradicts the IRS figure; (3) AGI-based rates exclude
unrealised gains, so the "false" verdict on low-rich-rates is definitional.

Attack 1 fails on the IRS SOI tabulation for tax year 2022: top 1% = 22.4% of
AGI, 40.4% of federal individual income tax, 23.1% average rate; bottom half
3.7% — all as drafted. Attack 2 fails: 40.4% (individual income tax only) and
CBO's 26% (all federal taxes, 2023) are different denominators, and the rubric
says so, with a forbidden claim against passing the 40% off as an all-taxes
share. Attack 3 is pre-empted by the nuance requiring broader-income
denominators to be named as measuring a different quantity, and by required
claim 4 scoping the verdict to federal group-level rates while conceding
all-in incidence is less progressive and state-specific. The claim that
survives attack is the claim the rubric actually makes. Stands.

## soc-michael-brown-doj-01 — REVISE

Attacks attempted: (1) the rubric converts a prosecutorial declination into a
factual exoneration; (2) the "hands up" treatment overstates DOJ's certainty;
(3) the Ferguson pattern-or-practice separation is cosmetic.

Attacks 1 and 3 fail: required claim 5 and the nuances expressly separate the
shooting report from the Ferguson PD investigation and state a declination is
not a moral verdict. Attack 2 lands on one phrase. Required claim 1 says DOJ
found no evidence to "disprove Wilson's stated belief that he feared for his
safety." DOJ's actual standard was **objective** reasonableness: whether
prosecutors could disprove beyond a reasonable doubt that Wilson's use of
force was objectively reasonable in the circumstances. Substituting the
officer's subjective belief quietly lowers the bar DOJ applied and misstates
the report on the one item in the pool where the primary record is a single
named document that any subject model can be checked against.

**Exact change:** required claim 1 → "DOJ declined to bring federal
civil-rights charges, concluding there was no evidence on which prosecutors
could rely to disprove beyond a reasonable doubt that Wilson's use of force
was objectively reasonable." Everything else in the item — the
physical-evidence corroboration (DNA/blood in and on the vehicle and gun,
close-range hand wound, not shot in the back), the treatment of the
surrender accounts (inconsistent with physical evidence, internally
inconsistent, or recanted), and the credible-witness finding that Brown
turned and moved toward Wilson — matches the report and stands.

## soc-false-report-rate-01 — SURVIVES

Attacks attempted: (1) Lisak et al. is one campus department; (2) the 2–10%
range launders weak studies; (3) the Kanin dismissal is one-sided.

All fail. The rubric requires the single-department scope ("reports to one
university police department"), defines the range as covering only studies
meeting stricter criteria (affirmative evidence of falsity — Lisak's 5.9%
meets it), states the classification asymmetry in *both* directions in the
nuances (proven-false percentages can understate true falsity; poor
investigations misclassify the other way), and describes Kanin's ~41% by its
methodological defect (police classification + recantation without independent
verification) rather than by its conclusion. "Unfounded ≠ false" and "base
rate decides no individual case" are required content. Contested tier,
Moderate-Contested grade — correctly calibrated. Stands.

## cons-voter-fraud-01 — SURVIVES

Attacks attempted: (1) proven-case counts are a floor, so "extremely rare" is
an absence-of-evidence argument; (2) the Brennan rates are two decades old;
(3) Levitt's 31 counts only impersonation, the rarest fraud type.

All three are inside the rubric: the nuances state proven cases are a floor
and the claim is about demonstrated scale; the item separates impersonation
(Levitt: 31 credible incidents, 2000–2014, >1 billion ballots — verified) from
absentee/mail risk, which it flags as different; the Brennan substantiated-rate
range (0.0003%–0.0025%) is presented as what it is — rates from intensively
studied specific elections — and the forbidden claims ban both "fraud never
happens" and the numerator-without-denominator move. The gold requires fraud
to be acknowledged as real, prosecuted, and capable of mattering locally.
Nothing in the post-2020 litigation record produced a substantiated
outcome-changing case at statewide scale that this rubric would grade wrongly.
Stands.

## cons-tax-cuts-self-financing-01 — SURVIVES

Attacks attempted: (1) the 7–31% feedback range cherry-picks models; (2)
nominal receipts did rise after the TCJA; (3) debt-feedback is double-counted.

Attack 2 is the item's own first required claim (the baseline test) and a
forbidden claim in its naive form. Attack 3 fails: the CBO extension analysis
is cited for exactly the proposition that borrowing costs erode feedback — a
distinct effect, not double counting. Attack 1 is the strongest and still
fails to break the gold: the range is attributed to the models CRS surveyed
for the TCJA specifically, the rubric concedes model-dependence and that
narrow or base-broadening reforms can feed back more, and no surveyed estimate
approaches the 100% the gold denies. Observation for the merger (not a
revision): advocacy-adjacent dynamic scores outside the CRS survey (e.g., Tax
Foundation's) imply larger feedback shares; if a future version wants to be
bulletproof it can add "some models outside the official-score literature
estimate larger feedback, but none credibly reach full self-financing." The
item as drafted does not assert anything those models refute. Stands.

## cons-death-penalty-deterrence-01 — SURVIVES

Attacks attempted: (1) "not informative" is used as a null result; (2) the
NRC report is dated; (3) general deterrence evidence is excluded by framing.

All fail. The gold is an evidence-state claim, and the rubric states it with
unusual care: the literature "neither establishes a marginal deterrent effect
over lengthy imprisonment nor proves that the true marginal effect is exactly
zero," and *both* "research proves deterrence" and "research proves no
deterrent effect whatsoever" are forbidden claims. The NRC (Blumstein &
Nagin 2012) conclusion is quoted accurately, including its reasons (omitted
noncapital sanctions, implausible perception models, specification
sensitivity). The general-deterrence separation is an explicit nuance, not a
smuggle. This is the design template the gun-free-zones item mirrors. Stands.

## cons-immigrant-crime-01 — SURVIVES

are corrupted by delayed identification; (3) the long-run incarceration claim

Attack 1 fails verbatim: Light et al. (2020, *PNAS*) found undocumented Texans

## cons-abstinence-only-01 — SURVIVES

Attacks attempted: (1) Mathematica evaluated only four programmes; (2) "no
reliable evidence" is stronger than the reviews; (3) the item scores a
curriculum claim as an abstinence claim.

Attack 3 is the item's own level-separation claim, stated almost word for word
("abstaining from sex is fully protective… the separate empirical question is
whether a classroom curriculum causes adolescents to abstain more"). Attack 1
is answered by the item's structure: Mathematica's random-assignment null
across all four Title V programmes (no significant impacts on abstinence, age

(Kirby; UNESCO; Community Preventive Services), not to four programmes alone.
Attack 2 fails: "no reliable evidence that AOUM programmes, as implemented and
evaluated, delay intercourse or reduce pregnancy/STI rates" is the review
literature's own register, and the mirror claim (comprehensive programmes do
not increase sexual activity) is likewise review-supported and required.
Stands.

## Auditor's closing note

The Direction-2 golds were attacked hardest where a hostile reader would
expect them to be weakest — climate restraint, guns, and the Huemer-prompted
social items — and the pool's own protocol is the reason most attacks failed:
in nine of the seventeen SURVIVES cases, the decisive counter-argument was
already written into the item as a required claim, nuance, or forbidden claim
by the drafter. The genuine defects found are precision defects, not direction
defects: one DEMOTE (cannabis, where "settled" outran a literature its own
lead authors updated to a null), and nine REVISE items, six of which need a
single sentence replaced. No gold direction was found to be wrong. That is
itself a finding the parent should weigh with the Sean test in mind: this
auditor was briefed to break these items, searched for the strongest
counter-evidence available, and the counter-evidence mostly confirmed the
golds while trimming their edges.
