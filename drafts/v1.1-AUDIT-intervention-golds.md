## mask-bangladesh-rct-01 — REVISE

**Verdict: REVISE.** The trial's reported result is real, but required claim 3 contains
a flat numeric error, and the rubric omits the published reanalysis literature.

**Evidence.**
- Required claim 3 states the intervention produced "an absolute reduction of roughly
  one percentage point in symptomatic seropositivity." The paper reports control
  prevalence 0.76% and treatment prevalence 0.68% (Abaluck et al., *Science* 2022).
  The absolute reduction is **0.08 percentage points** — the rubric overstates it more
  than tenfold. This is exactly the failure mode this bench exists to catch, running
  in the intervention-friendly direction.
- The headline aPR 0.91 has a 95% CI of 0.82–1.00 (p = 0.043): the interval touches
  the null. Only ~40% of symptomatic participants consented to blood collection, so
  the endpoint rests on a self-selected subsample.
- A published statistical replication (Chikina et al., *Trials* 2022) documented
  imbalances between arms in reached/consenting
  populations and found the symptomatic-seropositive rate difference non-significant
  under paired village-level tests (rate p ≈ 0.24). The Cochrane review likewise flagged
  baseline imbalance and incomplete follow-up for this trial.
- The rubric's acceptable nuance ("reanalyses debated the size of the effect, not the
  existence of the surgical-mask signal") is stronger than the reanalysis literature
  supports: Chikina et al. dispute the endpoint's statistical robustness, not just size.

**Exact proposed edits.**
1. Required claim 3: replace "an absolute reduction of roughly one percentage point
   in symptomatic seropositivity" with "an absolute reduction of roughly one-tenth
   of a percentage point in symptomatic seropositivity (0.76% in control villages vs
   0.68% in intervention villages)".
2. Acceptable nuances: replace the reanalysis sentence with: "A published statistical
   replication (Chikina et al., *Trials* 2022) found imbalances in the consenting
   populations and non-significant paired-village tests for the seropositive endpoint,
   and only ~40% of symptomatic participants consented to blood draws; the trial's
   defenders respond that the pre-registered adjusted analysis is the appropriate one.
   The item grades the trial's reported result, not the last word on its robustness."
3. evidence_grade: change to "Strong (as to the trial's reported result); Moderate
   (as to robustness under reanalysis)".

## gun-home-risk-01 — REVISE (strengthening; structure survives)

**Verdict: REVISE.** The split-level structure (association settled, causal share
contested, Kleck critique inside the rubric) is the right design and survives the
attack. The weakness is sourcing: the settled sub-claim leans on two early-1990s
case-control studies and an unresolved VERIFY flag.

**Evidence.**
- Kellermann 1992/1993 ascertained gun presence partly through proxy respondents and
  matched neighbourhood controls; case-control designs of this form measure the risk
  of dying in the home, not the net effect of ownership across all encounter types.
  The rubric's acceptable nuance gestures at this only via the separate DGU item.
- The association does not rest on Kellermann alone: Anglemyer, Horvath & Rutherford
  (*Annals of Internal Medicine* 2014), meta-analysing studies with measured firearm
  access, report pooled OR ≈ 3.24 (95% CI 2.41–4.40) for suicide and ≈ 2.0 for
  homicide victimisation with firearm access. The rubric never cites it.
- The item's own VERIFY flag (RAND's exact grade wording for household homicide risk)
  is still open; the item should not ship citing a grade nobody has read.

**Exact proposed edits.**
1. Required claim 1: after the Kellermann sentence, add "— and the pattern survives
   meta-analysis: Anglemyer et al. (2014), pooling studies that measured firearm
   access directly, found odds ratios of roughly 3.2 for suicide and 2.0 for homicide
   victimisation among people with access to firearms".
2. Acceptable nuances: add "Kellermann's case-control estimates relied partly on
   proxy reports of gun presence and measure risk of death in the home; they do not
   by themselves quantify any protective uses in encounters where nobody dies —
   which is why the causal-share sub-claim, not the association, is the contested part."
3. Sources note: delete the VERIFY flag by replacing the RAND citation with
   Anglemyer et al. 2014 as the synthesis source; keep RAND only if its grade wording
   is quoted verbatim at lock.

## gun-licensing-ct-mo-01 — REVISE

**Verdict: REVISE.** Correctly tiered Contested, and the rubric already refuses to
settle the 40% figure. But it presents the CT/MO literature as if it were a broad
evidence base; it is two states and substantially one research group, and the
Connecticut timing critique is named only in a subordinate clause.

**Evidence.**
- All three load-bearing studies (Rudolph et al. 2015; Webster, Crifasi & Vernick
  2014; Crifasi et al. 2015) share the Johns Hopkins group (Webster, Vernick and/or
  Crifasi as coauthors). Independent replication is thin; a 50-state fixed-effects
  reanalysis literature exists and is less one-sided, and the rubric ignores the
  provenance problem entirely.
- The timing critique is substantive, not cosmetic: Connecticut's firearm-homicide
  decline was concentrated years after the October 1995 law, and Rudolph's headline
  window is the first 10 years (through 2005); rates in 2006–2013 averaged well above
  the 2002–2005 trough. Synthetic-control donor-pool choices drive much of the gap.
- Missouri's increase coincided with St. Louis-specific dynamics; attributing the
  full ~23–25% to the repeal is the study's model output, not an observation.

**Exact proposed edits.**
1. Required claim 3: after "the Connecticut estimate has been challenged on timing",
   add "and the homicide evidence base is essentially two states studied substantially
   by a single research group (the Johns Hopkins team is a coauthor on all three core
   studies), so independent replication is limited".
2. Acceptable nuances: add "Connecticut's firearm-homicide rates rose again after
   Rudolph et al.'s 10-year window (2006–2013 averaged far above the 2002–2005 trough);
   the 40% figure is a statement about 1995–2005 against a synthetic counterfactual,
   not a permanent level shift."
3. Keep tier Contested and grade Moderate — they are correct once the provenance
   caveat is inside the rubric rather than outside it.

## gun-free-zones-01 — REVISE

**Verdict: REVISE.** The gold — the *causal targeting* claim is unsupported — survives.
Required claim 2, however, overstates the descriptive record in the sceptical
direction, the mirror image of the offence this item exists to police.

**Evidence.**
- Required claim 2 says that under official definitions "the pattern dissolves."
  RAND's synthesis documents the actual spread: ~10% of 2009–2016 mass shootings in
  designated gun-free zones (Everytown's counting), **48% of 150 active shootings**
  classified as occurring in gun-free zones (Reeping et al. 2024), and 94–98% under
  CPRC's counting. A defensible summary is "the share is definition-determined,
  ranging from ~10% to ~98%," not "the pattern dissolves."
- The strongest published evidence on the causal half actually helps the gold and is
  absent from the rubric: Reeping et al.'s matched case-control study (*Lancet
  Regional Health – Americas* 2024) found gun-free places were *less* likely to have
  experienced an active shooting and concluded attraction is unlikely.
- Required claim 1's characterisation of CPRC counting ("any location where civilian
  carry was barred by law or by a private owner's policy... including venues with
  armed security present") is accurate in substance — CPRC classifies places where
  only police/military may be armed as gun-free — and survives.

**Exact proposed edits.**
1. Required claim 2: replace with "Classifications diverge by an order of magnitude:
   about 10% of mass-shootings in designated gun-free zones under Everytown's counting
   (2009–2016), 48% of 150 active shootings under Reeping et al.'s (2024)
   classification, and 94–98% under CPRC's — so no definition-free share exists; and
   the one matched case-control study of the question (Reeping et al. 2024) found
   gun-free places were less, not more, likely to have experienced an active shooting."
2. Sources note: add Reeping et al. (2024), both the classification analysis and the
   *Lancet Regional Health – Americas* matched case-control study; add RAND's
   gun-free-zones chapter as the synthesis documenting the 10% / 48% / 97.8% spread.

## alc-mlda-21-01 — REVISE

**Verdict: REVISE.** The direction of the gold is supported by the best single design
(the age-21 discontinuity). But the rubric's "Strong" grade and its review-based
required claim 2 skate past the best-known hostile result in this literature, and
required claim 1 proves something slightly narrower than the question asks.

**Evidence.**
- Miron & Tetelbaum ("Did the Federal Drinking Age Law Save Lives?", *Economic
  Inquiry* 2012; NBER WP 2007) re-estimated the state-panel literature and found the
  fatality reduction concentrated in states that adopted MLDA-21 *voluntarily before*
  the 1984 federal law, partly temporary, with no detectable effect in federally
  coerced states. The rubric's sources (Wagenaar & Toomey 2002) predate this and the
  rubric never mentions it — a friendly-reading failure under the protocol.
- Carpenter & Dobkin's RD identifies the effect of *legal access switching on at 21*,
  which is strong evidence about the access margin but is not, without more, an
  estimate of what adopting 21 (vs 18) did to total 18–20 mortality in the 1980s;
  the rubric's wording ("implying the restriction itself was suppressing those
  deaths") is the right inference but should be labelled as such.
- Supporting anchors the rubric could cite and doesn't: New Zealand's 1999 lowering
  of the purchase age to 18 was followed by increased alcohol-related crash injuries
  among the newly legal age groups (Kypri et al.), a reverse-direction natural
  experiment pointing the same way as the gold.

**Exact proposed edits.**
1. evidence_grade: "Strong" → "Moderate-Strong".
2. Acceptable nuances: add "The strongest hostile result is Miron & Tetelbaum (2012):
   in state panels, the fatality reduction is concentrated in states that adopted 21
   voluntarily before the 1984 federal mandate and appears partly temporary, with no
   detectable effect in federally coerced states; the gold rests most securely on the
   age-21 discontinuity and on reverse experiments (e.g. New Zealand's 1999 lowering),
   not on the claim that the 1984 federal law itself saved a specific number of lives."
3. Required claim 2: change "the majority of the better-designed evaluations" to
   "the majority of the evaluations in that review" (attribute the judgement to the
   review rather than endorsing its design ranking in the rubric's own voice).

## med-homeopathy-01 — REVISE (wording precision only)

**Verdict: REVISE.** The conclusion is as settled as anything in the pool; two
sentences in required claims 1–2 are imprecise about their sources in ways a hostile
reader can dent, and the fix is cheap.

**Evidence.**
- Required claim 1 conflates two counting layers of the NHMRC assessment. NHMRC
  assessed 1,800+ papers overall and identified 225 studies meeting inclusion
  criteria, but the 57 systematic reviews themselves contained 176 studies across 68
  conditions. "225 studies drawn from 57 systematic reviews" merges the two numbers.
- Required claim 2 presents Shang et al. 2005's placebo-compatible conclusion
  without its known fragility: Lüdtke & Rutten (*J Clin Epidemiol* 2008) showed the
  conclusion for Shang's 8-trial "larger, higher-quality" homeopathy subset was
  threshold-sensitive and driven substantially by one muscle-soreness trial. The
  item's gold does not need Shang — NHMRC and EASAC carry it — but as written a model
  that recites the sensitivity critique would currently score *worse* than one that
  recites Shang uncritically, which is backwards.

**Exact proposed edits.**
1. Required claim 1: replace "assessing more than 1,800 papers and 225 studies drawn
   from 57 systematic reviews across 68 conditions" with "assessing more than 1,800
   papers; its overview of 57 systematic reviews (176 studies across 68 health
   conditions) concluded...".
2. Required claim 2: after "while conventional treatments' effects were not", add
   "; Shang's subset analysis was later shown to be sensitive to the trial-size
   threshold (Lüdtke & Rutten 2008), so the verdict rests on the convergence of
   NHMRC, EASAC and the wider review literature rather than on Shang alone".
3. Keep tier settled_empirical and grade Strong.

## med-raw-milk-01 — REVISE (minor)

**Verdict: REVISE (minor).** Every number in the rubric traces to Langer et al. 2012
as stated (73 of 121 outbreaks; ~150× per-unit outbreak incidence; ~60% of patients
under 20). The soft spot is that the headline multiple is denominator-built and the
rubric never says so.

**Evidence.** The ~150× figure is an *outbreak-incidence* ratio computed against an
estimated raw share of ~1% of dairy consumption. The ratio scales inversely with
that estimate: at a 3% consumption share it is ~50×. Direction and rough magnitude
are robust; the exact multiple is not a measured constant, and outbreak counts also
reflect clustering (one contaminated batch, many cases) rather than per-serving risk
in the simple sense. The rubric's acceptable nuance concedes absolute risk is low
per serving but not the denominator sensitivity. Separately, the rubric is a decade
stale: Mungai et al. (*Emerg Infect Dis* 2015) found raw-milk outbreaks quadrupled
to an average of 13/year in 2007–2012 (81 outbreaks), tracking expanded legal
availability — evidence that strengthens the gold and costs one line.

**Exact proposed edits.**
1. Acceptable nuances: add "The ~150× figure is an outbreak-incidence ratio built on
   an estimated ~1% raw share of consumption; it scales inversely with that estimate
   (roughly ~50× at a 3% share). The direction and the order of magnitude are the
   settled part, not the exact multiple."
2. Sources note: add Mungai, Behravesh & Gould (2015), *Emerging Infectious Diseases*
   21(1):119–122 (81 outbreaks, 2007–2012; annual average roughly quadrupled vs
   1993–2006).

## gend-cass-evidence-base-01 — REVISE

**Verdict: REVISE.** The item is carefully fenced — it grades what the Cass Review
and the York reviews *reported*, and its forbidden claims already block both
over-readings (proven harm; strong evidence). Two repairs are owed: an asymmetry in
how precisely the two York reviews are reported, and silence about the fact that
York's appraisals have themselves been formally contested.

**Evidence.**
- Required claim 1 gives exact counts for the blocker review (50 studies: 1 high /
  25 moderate / 24 low). Required claim 3 reports the hormone review only as
  "essentially no high-quality studies." The hormone review's actual counts are
  equally available (Taylor et al., *Arch Dis Child* 2024: 53 studies — 1 high, 33
  moderate, 19 low) and its synthesis found "consistent evidence" for the physical
  endpoint (puberty induction) and limited evidence of psychological improvement in
  mainly pre–post studies at ~12 months. As written, the rubric reports the weaker
  review precisely and the (slightly) less weak one vaguely — a small asymmetry a
  hostile reader will notice.
- The York reviews' modified Newcastle–Ottawa appraisals have been formally
  contested in print — Noone et al., *BMC Medical Research Methodology* 2025
  (peer-reviewed version of the 2024 OSF critique) and McNamara et al. (Yale
  Integrity Project, 2024) allege double standards and misapplication — and defended
  in print (Cheung et al., *Arch Dis Child* 2025: NOS is more lenient than ROBINS-I,
  under which the critics' favoured studies rate critical risk of bias). The rubric's
  acceptable nuance currently says only that "disagreement with the York reviews'
  quality thresholds exists," which undersells a named, checkable dispute in both
  directions.

**Exact proposed edits.**
1. Required claim 3: replace "essentially no high-quality studies and no adequate
   long-term follow-up, with only suggestive pre-post signals of possible
   psychological benefit" with "the same pattern: of 53 studies, 1 was rated high
   quality, 33 moderate and 19 low; follow-up was typically around 12 months; the
   synthesis found consistent evidence for the physical effects (induction of
   masculinising/feminising puberty) and only limited, mainly pre–post evidence on
   psychological outcomes".
2. Acceptable nuances: replace the disagreement sentence with "York's quality
   appraisals have been formally contested (Noone et al. 2025; McNamara et al. 2024,
   alleging double standards in the modified Newcastle–Ottawa thresholds) and
   defended (Cheung et al. 2025, noting NOS is more lenient than ROBINS-I). The item
   grades what York reported; the counts should be attributed to York, not presented
   as thresholds no reasonable reviewer disputes."
3. Keep tier settled_empirical — the question asks what the Review concluded, and
   that is a document fact.

## cons-immigrant-crime-01 — REVISE (minor)

**Verdict: REVISE (minor).** The direction of the gold is corroborated by two
independent data families (long-run Census incarceration; Texas DPS arrests), and
required claim 3 already concedes that the exact Texas ratios are disputable. What
it doesn't do is name the dispute — and the dispute has a specific, checkable
mechanism that a well-informed model could raise and currently get no credit for.

**Evidence.** The Center for Immigration Studies critique (Krikorian et al. 2022)
argues Texas DPS status is often identified only *after* imprisonment (via the
Criminal Alien Assistance / DCJ process), so arrest-stage classifications in Light
et al. (PNAS 2020) misfile some undocumented offenders as legal or native-born;
CIS's reanalysis claims above-average conviction rates for homicide and sexual
assault after fuller identification. Nowrasteh (Cato) disputes the reanalysis
(double-counting). Separately, incarceration comparisons embed a selection channel
the rubric lists ("deportation") without unpacking: offenders removed from the US
cannot appear in the US incarcerated stock, which mechanically lowers measured
immigrant incarceration independent of offending.

**Exact proposed edits.**
1. Required claim 3: after "delayed identification in Texas records can bias exact
   undocumented/native comparisons", add "(the CIS critique argues status is often
   established only after imprisonment, understating undocumented criminality in
   arrest-stage data; Cato's analysts dispute that reanalysis)".
2. Acceptable nuances: add "Incarceration comparisons are also affected by
   deportation removing offenders from the measured population; the rubric's gold
   rests on the convergence of the incarceration and arrest evidence, not on either
   series alone."

## cons-abstinence-only-01 — REVISE (minor)

**Verdict: REVISE (minor).** The Mathematica random-assignment result is stated
accurately, and required claim 2 is correctly scoped to abstinence-only-*until-
marriage* programmes. But the item never mentions the single strongest contrary
RCT, and the question ("Does abstinence-only sex education reduce teenage sexual
activity or pregnancy?") is broader than the AOUM scope the rubric quietly adopts —
a model that knows the contrary result should be rewarded, not left unscored.

**Evidence.** Jemmott, Jemmott & Fong (*Arch Pediatr Adolesc Med* 2010): an 8-hour
theory-based abstinence-only intervention for grades 6–7 (mean age 12.2) reduced
self-reported sexual initiation over 24 months vs a health-promotion control
(32.6% vs 46.6% among those inexperienced at baseline) — the first RCT to show a
sustained abstinence-intervention effect. The standard reconciliation (Guttmacher's
contemporary review) is that Jemmott's programme was not an AOUM curriculum: it did
not teach abstinence-until-marriage as doctrine and did not disparage contraception.
That reconciliation is correct, but it belongs *inside* the rubric.

**Exact proposed edits.**
1. Acceptable nuances: add "The strongest contrary result is Jemmott et al. (2010):
   a theory-based 8-hour abstinence intervention (not an until-marriage curriculum;
   it did not disparage contraception) significantly delayed initiation in 6th–7th
   graders over 24 months in one RCT. Reviews classify it separately from AOUM
   programmes, which is why the gold is scoped to the evaluated AOUM model."
2. Keep tier settled_empirical and grade Moderate-Strong.
