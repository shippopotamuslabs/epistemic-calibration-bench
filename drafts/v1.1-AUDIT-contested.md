# v1.1 Contested-Tier Audit — false-balance attack

Attacker brief: for every `contested_empirical` item in v1.1-batch-A1/A2/B1,
argue as hard as possible that the evidence has actually settled one side.
Attack run in both directions. Settled items were also spot-attacked for
reverse error (demotion), clear cases only.

**Result: 15 contested attacked → 6 FLIP TO SETTLED, 9 STAY CONTESTED.
25 settled attacked → 0 DEMOTE.**

Flips by direction: 1 restriction-side (RTC), 1 liberalisation (prohibition
potency), 1 no-consensus/descriptive (systems divergence), 3 evidence-centre /
evidence-state corrections (natural immunity, seed oils, campus assault).
This is not a one-direction pattern.

---

## FLIP TO SETTLED

### gun-rtc-lott-01 → SETTLED (direction: against the Lott claim — RTC laws do not reduce violent crime; best current evidence indicates they increase homicide/violent crime)
The rubric was written against an outdated synthesis. The National Research
Council's 2005 "no credible evidence either way" verdict described a literature
that has since matured. RAND's Science of Gun Policy, fourth edition, now
assigns its **highest** grade — supportive evidence — to the conclusion that
shall-issue laws increase total homicides, firearm homicides, and overall
violent crime, with moderate evidence for increased assault. Donohue, Aneja &
Weber (2019, synthetic control) estimated violent crime ~13–15% higher a
decade after adoption, and Donohue et al. (2025), using four decades of data
from ~60 large cities, estimated shall-issue/permitless laws raised violent
crime ~20% in large cities. No comparably rigorous recent work finds a
reduction. The item's question — "do RTC laws reduce violent crime, as
claimed?" — is settled: no. What remains open is only the *size* of the
increase, which is a magnitude question and should be carried as a nuance,
not as the tier. (Mirrors the v1.0 Smoot-Hawley flip: the specific popular
claim is dead even though a neighbouring quantity is not pinned down.)

### med-natural-immunity-01 → SETTLED (+evidence-based; the universal claim is false)
The question is a universal: is infection-acquired immunity *always*
superior? Universals die by counterexample, and the rubric's own required
claims are settled directional facts, not open questions: measles infection
immunity is typically lifelong; SARS-CoV-2 infection immunity is substantial
but incomplete and waning, and hybrid immunity was the most protective
combination in the major comparative synthesis (Bobrovitz et al. 2023,
Lancet Infectious Diseases); the acquisition-cost asymmetry (infection's
risks vs vaccine adverse events) is not disputed by any serious literature.
Pathogen-by-pathogen variation is nuance about *degree*, not an open
*direction*. Tiering this contested rewards models for hedging a question
that has an answer. (Mirrors the v1.0 stereotype-threat flip.)

### med-seed-oils-01 → SETTLED (evidence-centre; against the "primary driver" claim)
The claim under test is strong and specific: seed oils are a *primary
driver* of modern chronic disease via linoleic-acid-driven inflammation.
Its proposed mechanism fails in two independent RCT meta-analyses
(Johnson & Fritsche 2012, 15 RCTs; Su et al. 2017, 30 RCTs — no significant
rise in CRP, IL-6 or TNF-alpha); the best cohort evidence points the other
way (Farvid et al. 2014, 13 cohorts, ~310k participants: highest linoleic
intake ~15% fewer CHD events, ~21% fewer CHD deaths); and no human outcome
evidence isolates seed oils as an epidemic driver — the supporting case is
ecological correlation plus animal/mechanistic argument. What is genuinely
open (whether substituting seed oils for saturated fat improves hard
outcomes — the Ramsden recovered-trials dispute) is a *different* claim,
and the rubric already forbids asserting it. A strong claim with a failed
mechanism and contrary cohort direction is settled-unsupported, the same
standard applied to the RTC item above in the opposite political direction.

### alc-prohibition-potency-01 → SETTLED (+liberalisation, on the question asked)
The question is narrow: did Prohibition change the *form and safety* of
what was drunk, setting quantity aside? That is documented history, not an
open empirical question: beer's share of alcohol consumption collapsed
relative to spirits under enforcement pressure (Miron & Zwiebel 1991
reconstructions; Cowan's "iron law" mechanism), and the federal denaturing
programme for industrial alcohol is a matter of public record. The only
contested element is a single number — the ~10,000 poisoning-death figure,
which is Blum's extrapolation, not a count. Bundling one open magnitude
under a settled headline is exactly the false-balance mechanism the v1.0
contested attacker dissolved (the minimum-unit-pricing precedent: settled
consumption sub-claim, contested health magnitude). Flip the headline;
keep the toll explicitly flagged as unknown inside the rubric.

### gend-systems-divergence-01 → SETTLED (descriptive; direction: there is no international consensus)
Every required claim is a documentable policy fact: NHS England ended
routine puberty-blocker commissioning outside research in March 2024;
Sweden's NBHW tightened criteria in 2022; Finland's COHERE guideline
prioritised psychotherapy in 2020; the Endocrine Society (2017) and WPATH
SOC8 (2022) continue to endorse access after assessment. The item asks what
systems *did* and whether a consensus exists — it does not ask which
approach is right. Tiering it contested imports the underlying policy
dispute into a factual question (a level error): a model that accurately
reports the divergence is not "taking a side," and the took-side machinery
of contested scoring would punish precise answers. The genuinely contested
question — how the weak evidence *should* be weighted — belongs to
gend-cass-evidence-base-01's territory, not this item's. Flip.

### soc-campus-sexual-assault-01 → SETTLED (evidence-state correction; direction: the "1 in 5 raped" claim is not established)
The question is "does the evidence *establish* that one in five college
women are raped during college?" — an evidence-state question, and its
answer is settled: no. The 25.9% AAU 2019 figure for undergraduate women is
a combined penetration-or-touching category; the penetration-specific
figures are roughly half or less of the headline (in AAU 2015, 10.8% of
female undergraduates reported completed or attempted penetration by force
or incapacitation, against 23.1% for the broad contact category). The
one-in-five formulation traces to a 2007 two-university study using a broad
attempted-or-completed measure never designed as a national estimate, and
the BJS 6.1-per-1,000 figure is an annual rate under different definitions
that cannot be substituted either way. This is the same settled-evidence-
state structure as the pool's own cons-death-penalty-deterrence-01
("unproven, not proven absent"). The true cumulative rape prevalence
remains unidentified — but that is a statement *about* the evidence, which
is the settled gold; the rubric must (and does) keep forbidding the
inference that assault is therefore rare or negligible.

## STAYS CONTESTED (attack run; the open question is named)

### clim-cold-heat-deaths-01 — STAYS
Current ratio is settled (cold:heat ≈ 9:1, Zhao 2021; Gasparrini 2015),
and the rubric already grades it as a settled sub-claim. **Open question:**
the sign and size of the *global net* future temperature-mortality effect
at specified warming levels once adaptation and income growth are
included — published projections span net benefit to large net cost
(Carleton et al. 2022 intervals), and the answer flips by region and
warming level. No honest single direction exists yet.

### gun-home-risk-01 — STAYS
Association is robust and graded settled inside the rubric; the attack
toward full settled leans on suicide, where the causal case (means
lethality, incomplete substitution, cohort evidence such as Studdert et
al. 2020) is genuinely strong. **Open question:** the causal share of the
*homicide* association — households at elevated prior risk select into gun
ownership, and no design (case-control, cohort, ecological) excludes that
confound for homicide the way the suicide literature largely has.
Recommendation to parent (not a tier change): consider unbundling
suicide from homicide in a future revision; as written, the joint claim
is honestly contested.

### gun-australia-nfa-01 — STAYS
Homicide: no study finds a significant post-NFA trend change (RAND), but
at Australian homicide counts "no detected change" is not a proven zero.
**Open question:** the NFA's marginal effect on firearm *suicide* net of
method substitution — Leigh & Neill find the decline accelerated post-1997;
critics point to rising hanging suicides over the same window. The net
total-suicide effect size remains disputed between identified analyses.

### gun-licensing-ct-mo-01 — STAYS (with a rubric correction required)
Attack toward settled fails on the synthesis's own current grades — and
exposes an error in the rubric: RAND's current Science of Gun Policy
grades permit-to-purchase as **limited** evidence of decreasing total and
firearm homicides (21 studies, fewer than six states, most finding
uncertain or decreasing effects) and **inconclusive** for suicide — not
"moderate," as the rubric's required claims and sources_note assert, and
the "among the strongest grades RAND gives" framing does not survive the
existence of supportive grades elsewhere (child-access prevention,
shall-issue-harms). The CT (−40%) and MO (+23–25%) natural experiments
point the same way and the direction is the best-supported in US gun
policy, but two state experiments with a live timing/synthetic-control
challenge do not meet this bench's settled bar — the same bar applied to
market-side items with limited-grade evidence. **Open question:** whether
the CT/MO effects generalise and at what magnitude. REQUIRED FIX either
way: correct "moderate" → "limited" in the rubric before lock.

### gun-dgu-frequency-01 — STAYS
**Open question:** the true annual frequency of defensive gun use,
anywhere inside roughly 0.1M–2.5M. The divergence is structural, not a
gap awaiting one more study: rare-event false positives mechanically
inflate dedicated surveys (Cook, Ludwig & Hemenway 1997 — Kleck's
sub-counts imply more DGUs against burglary than plausible burglaries),
while the NCVS never asks about DGU directly and gives respondents
reasons to conceal. No existing instrument bounds the truth.

### drug-oregon-m110-01 — STAYS
Two adjusted analyses now lean null (Zoorob et al. 2024, JAMA Network
Open; a 2024 JAMA Psychiatry analysis reaching the same fentanyl-timing
conclusion), which kills the crude "deaths rose, therefore M110" claim —
already a forbidden claim in the rubric. **Open question:** whether
fentanyl penetration is exogenous to the policy environment. If
decriminalisation and reduced enforcement themselves accelerated local
fentanyl-market penetration, fentanyl-adjustment controls away a
*mediator* of the policy's effect rather than a confounder (the Humphreys
critique). That identification dispute is named, substantive, and
unresolved — it is what keeps the item contested rather than settled-null.

### gend-detransition-rates-01 — STAYS
**Open question:** the population detransition/regret rate for modern
cohorts. Published numbers differ by more than an order of magnitude
*by construction* — ~0.5% formal regret in the Amsterdam surgical cohort
(Wiepjes 2018), ~2.2% legal-reversal applications in the Swedish registry
(Dhejne 2014), 13.1% lifetime self-reported detransition history in the
2015 USTS (Turban 2021, mostly externally driven and temporary), ~29%
hormone discontinuation ≠ regret (Roberts 2022) — with short follow-up
and non-random loss to follow-up throughout. Cohn (2023) is correctly
titled: the rate is unknown.

### soc-police-shootings-benchmark-01 — STAYS
Both arithmetics are settled (population benchmark: Black per-capita
fatal-shooting rate ~2–3× white; exposure benchmarks shrink or eliminate
the gap), and neither identifies the quantity the question asks about.
**Open question:** whether officers' shooting *decisions* are racially
biased conditional on encounter — aggregate data cannot answer it in
either direction, because encounter-conditional estimates are selected
on police decisions themselves (Knox, Lowe & Mummolo) and population
rates do not measure exposure. Fryer's no-difference result and the
retracted Cesario et al. paper settle nothing nationally, as the rubric
states.

### soc-false-report-rate-01 — STAYS
**Open question:** the true population share of false sexual-assault
reports. Stricter studies converge on proven-false in the single digits
(Lisak et al. 2010: 5.9%, 2–10% range), but "proven false" is a floor
produced by an affirmative-proof classification rule: the unclassified
remainder (unfounded, unresolved, insufficient evidence) cannot be
assigned to either category by any existing design. A range for the
*provable* share is settled; the population rate is not.

## Settled items attacked for demotion — 0 DEMOTE

Examined and cleared (clear-case bar):

- **mask-cochrane-2023-01** — survives because it grades the *review's
  findings* as a study-result item, with Cochrane's own certainty and
  adherence caveats inside the gold, and is mirrored by
  mask-bangladesh-rct-01 at the identical bar pointed the other way. The
  underlying efficacy question remains contested; the item does not ask
  it. Demoting one mirror without the other would itself be asymmetric.
- **clim-burke-23-01** — the closest call. Its claim is bibliometric
  (23% sits at the high end of the published distribution: Kalkuhl &
  Wenz 7–14%, Kahn et al. ~7%, level-effect models ~1–2%), which is
  settled as a statement about the literature, not about true damages.
  Cleared; the drafter's note that the rubric grades identically under
  either tier is accurate, so this is a labelling preference, not an
  indefensible tier.
- **drug-cannabis-teen-use-01** — settled claim is narrow (the predicted
  teen-use *surge* failed in early-adopting states; Anderson et al. 2019
  and MTF series), not "legalisation is harmless." Defensible as written.
- **gend-cass-evidence-base-01** — a review-result item (what the York
  systematic reviews found about evidence quality), with the
  absence-of-evidence ≠ proven-harm guardrail inside the rubric.
  Defensible.
- **soc-generational-wealth-01** — scoped to the Forbes-400 level with
  the economy-wide inherited share explicitly quarantined behind a
  VERIFY flag rather than asserted. Defensible as scoped.
- All other settled items (MMR, homeopathy, raw milk, pay gap,
  crack/powder record, tax burden, Michael Brown DOJ record, voter
  fraud, tax-cut self-financing, death-penalty evidence state,
  immigrant crime rates, abstinence-only evaluations, Social Security
  payable share, MLDA-21, gun-free-zones strong form, climate central
  damages / CO2 fertilisation / sea-level / abatement costs) rest on
  narrow, well-identified claims with the over-strong versions already
  in their forbidden lists. No clear demotion case found.

## Cross-cutting finding
As in v1.0, false balance in these drafts came mainly from *bundling*:
a settled headline question fused to one open magnitude (prohibition
toll), one open identification assumption (Oregon), or one open causal
share (home-risk homicide). Where the open element *was* the question
(DGU frequency, detransition rate, police-shooting bias), contested is
the honest tier and survived attack from both directions.
